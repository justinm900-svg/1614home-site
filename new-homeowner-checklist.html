Your First 90 Days in a New House

You just got the keys. Congratulations. The first few months in a central Ohio house go better if you know where the shutoffs are, get a clean filter in, and find out whether the sump actually runs. Here's the short list I'd give a neighbor in Hilliard, Dublin, Plain City, Upper Arlington, or anywhere in Columbus.

  1. 1

    Find the shutoffs and the panel

    • Find the main water shutoff and try it gently. If it turns, put a tag on it or a note on the wall so anyone in the house can find it. If it's stuck or looks old, leave it alone and write that down. Don't force it.
    • Find the electrical panel. Open the door so you know where it is in the dark.
    • If you have gas, find that shutoff too.
    • Take a photo of each one. Future you will be glad.
  2. 2

    Swap the HVAC filter and write down your equipment

    • You don't know when the last filter was changed, so change it now. The size is printed on the edge. The airflow arrow points toward the furnace.
    • Write the date on the new filter. Most 1 inch filters need changing every 1 to 3 months, sooner with pets.
    • Look at the labels on the furnace, the AC, and the water heater. Write down the model numbers and the year they were made, if you can find it.
  3. 3

    Test every smoke and CO alarm

    • Press the test button on every alarm, on every floor. You want one on each level and outside the bedrooms.
    • Look for the date on the back. Smoke alarms are good for about 10 years. CO alarms usually 7 to 10. Replace any that are past it.
    • Swap batteries in anything that chirps. If a CO alarm goes off, get everyone outside and call 911.
  4. 4

    Water: the sump, the water heater, and the usual leak spots

    • If you have a sump pump, pour water in the pit and make sure the pump runs and drains. Do this before the first big rain, not after.
    • Look at the water heater and the floor around it for rust, drips, or a missing drain pan.
    • Peek under the kitchen and bathroom sinks, behind the washer, and at the fridge ice line.
    • After a hard rain, walk the outside of the house. Downspouts should send water 4 to 6 feet away from the foundation, not right next to it.
  5. 5

    A few more that are easy to skip

    • Check the dryer vent flap on the outside of the house for lint buildup.
    • Look at the caulk around tubs and showers. Cracks are how water gets behind the tile.
    • If you move in during fall or winter, pull the hoses off the outside faucets, drain the lines, and put an insulated cover on each one. Close the inside shutoffs if you have them.
    • Buy a spare furnace filter in the right size and leave it on the shelf.

While you're at it

Keep a simple note on your phone: shutoff locations, filter size, and the ages of the furnace, AC, and water heater. That's the start of a Home Record, and it makes every later decision easier. If anything looks wrong, or water is coming in, call a licensed pro.
