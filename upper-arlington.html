What I see in Upper Arlington homes

Every house is different, but the same few things come up again and again in Upper Arlington.

  • Standard outside faucets with a shutoff valve inside.Older UA houses usually have a short spigot outside and a valve on the inside wall, often a stop and waste valve with a small bleeder cap. Winterizing means closing that valve, draining the line, and leaving the outside faucet open. Skipping it is how a pipe splits inside the wall.
  • Shutoff valves that have not been turned in decades.Gate valves from the 1950s and 1960s seize. On the first visit I find the main, see whether it moves without forcing it, tag it, and note it for a plumber if it needs replacing. I do not force an old valve.
  • Houses with no sump pump at all, and some with one added later.Many older basements drain to a floor drain rather than a pit. Where a pump was added during a finish or a waterproofing job, it is often tucked in a closet and forgotten. Either way, the water heater and any laundry on an upper floor get a leak sensor.
  • Alarms past their replacement date.Smoke alarms are meant to be replaced every ten years and CO alarms every five to seven. Older houses tend to have a mix of ages. Every visit, each one gets tested and any past its date gets written down.
